\title{The Effect of Prototyping on Identifying Missing and Unclear Requirements: A Systematic Literature Review}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Nguyen Pham Xuan Nhi}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Department of Software Engineering, FPT University} \\
Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam \\
Email: nhinpxse201170@fpt.edu.vn}
}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Requirements Engineering (RE) is widely recognized as a critical phase in the software development lifecycle, yet it remains profoundly vulnerable to defects stemming from human cognitive limitations, tacit knowledge, and linguistic ambiguity in natural language specifications. Prototyping is frequently proposed as an experiential catalyst to reveal unspoken needs and clarify ambiguous statements. This paper presents a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) investigating the empirical effect of prototyping on identifying missing and unclear requirements. By synthesizing 26 peer-reviewed foundational, empirical, and seminal studies, we analyze: (1) how prototypes are operationalized across elicitation and validation under the Prototyping Assessment Model (PAM) and cognitive filtering theory; (2) empirical evidence evaluating whether prototypes assist stakeholders in identifying missing or unclear requirements better than textual specifications alone; and (3) requirement typologies (functional, usability/UI, and non-functional quality attributes) clarified most effectively through visual, in-situ, and video-based modalities. Furthermore, we examine critical trade-offs regarding high-fidelity prototypes (premature idea cementing), GenAI-assisted rapid prototyping, and practical agile integration (Enriched User Stories) to mitigate late-stage rework. Finally, open research gaps and proposed research directions grounded in empirical pilot testing are articulated.
\end{abstract}
