\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This Systematic Literature Review synthesized 26 peer-reviewed foundational, seminal, and empirical studies (partitioned into 15 core empirical investigations and 11 foundational works) investigating the effect of prototyping on identifying missing and unclear requirements in software engineering. Grounded in IREB principles and three analytical dimensions (rationale, operational protocol, and taxonomical classification), our findings provide evidence-based answers to the three research questions:

First, regarding \textit{prototyping operationalization across elicitation and validation (RQ6.1)}, prototypes operationalized under the PAM framework \cite{bjarnason2023empirically} and Lim et al.'s \cite{lim2008anatomy} filtering model function as shared cognitive artifacts. In elicitation, prototypes surface tacit knowledge and automated GUI tools act as cognitive stimulators for self-elicitation ($MAP = 0.741$) \cite{kolthoff2024selfelicitation}. In validation, hands-on interaction catalyzes requirements evolution, uncovering up to 42\% additional missing requirements \cite{rej2022evolve}.

Second, regarding \textit{comparative efficacy over textual specifications and interviews (RQ6.2)}, prototyping significantly outperforms textual specifications and unstructured interviews in gross functional requirement discovery volume ($p = 0.02$) \cite{rueda2020requirements} and provides a gender-egalitarian medium ($p = 0.132$) \cite{diaz2021gender}. Automated GUI synthesis \cite{wei2023boosting} slashes the historical 145-minute fabrication bottleneck to seconds, while automated LLM pipelines achieve 85.2\% accuracy in interlinking agile user stories to UI component trees \cite{kolthoff2024interlinking}, effectively counteracting agile minimal documentation risks \cite{inayat2015systematic}.

Third, regarding \textit{efficacy across requirement typologies (RQ6.3)}, prototypes excel at resolving ambiguous functional workflows and visual usability defects ($p = 0.000$) \cite{uv2023empirical}. The traditional non-functional requirement deficit of passive mockups is resolved through augmented modalities, including think-aloud paper prototyping ($p = 0.001$) \cite{abad2018loud}, contextual video scenarios \cite{bano2023videos}, and in-situ physical testing that exposes real-time safety and latency constraints in cyber-physical systems \cite{hoffmann2011automotive}.

In conclusion, prototyping is an indispensable, empirically validated discipline for resolving unconscious disambiguation \cite{berry2003contract} and eliminating requirement omissions. To maximize efficacy while mitigating premature idea cementing, teams should deploy low-fidelity sketches during exploratory framing, adopt Enriched User Stories during backlog grooming, leverage standardized rubrics \cite{lending2022rubric} to foster interactive co-design, and transition to high-fidelity mockups only during confirmatory pre-release validation.
