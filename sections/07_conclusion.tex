\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This Systematic Literature Review synthesized 26 peer-reviewed studies (15 core empirical investigations and 11 foundational works) examining the effect of prototyping on identifying missing and unclear requirements. 

Across the three research questions, the literature demonstrates that prototyping plays distinct roles across the RE lifecycle (RQ6.1). In elicitation, visual and interactive mockups prompt stakeholders to uncover tacit knowledge, with recent automated GUI tools supporting user self-elicitation ($MAP = 0.741$) \cite{kolthoff2024selfelicitation}. In validation, hands-on interaction allows stakeholders to trace operational flows, uncovering up to 42\% additional missing requirements \cite{rej2022evolve}.

Comparatively (RQ6.2), prototyping significantly outperforms textual specifications and unstructured interviews in functional requirement discovery volume ($p = 0.02$) \cite{rueda2020requirements} while offering a balanced collaborative setting across participants ($p = 0.132$) \cite{diaz2021gender}. Emerging generative AI tools \cite{wei2023boosting} substantially reduce initial mockup creation time, and automated traceability pipelines \cite{kolthoff2024interlinking} help connect agile user stories with evolving UI components, mitigating documentation gaps \cite{inayat2015systematic}.

Regarding requirement typologies (RQ6.3), prototyping is particularly effective for functional requirements and usability constraints ($p = 0.000$) \cite{uv2023empirical}. While passive visual mockups struggle to surface non-functional requirements, augmented techniques such as think-aloud paper prototyping ($p = 0.001$) \cite{abad2018loud}, contextual video scenarios \cite{bano2023videos}, and in-situ physical testing \cite{hoffmann2011automotive} effectively bridge this gap.

Overall, prototyping provides a practical, empirically supported approach to reduce requirement omissions and linguistic ambiguities \cite{berry2003contract}. To balance cost and benefit, development teams can use low-fidelity sketches during early discovery, attach wireframes to user stories during backlog grooming, apply structured rubrics \cite{lending2022rubric} to foster collaborative elicitation, and reserve high-fidelity prototypes for confirmatory validation before implementation.
