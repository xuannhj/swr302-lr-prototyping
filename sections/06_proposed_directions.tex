\section{Proposed directions and practical validation}
\label{sec:directions}

To address the research gaps identified in Section~\ref{sec:gaps}, this section outlines a streamlined workflow integrating prototyping into agile backlog grooming, followed by a preliminary pilot validation on an undergraduate capstone project.

\subsection{Proposed agile backlog grooming workflow}
In agile software engineering, minimal textual specifications frequently lead to unstated assumptions, linguistic ambiguities, and costly sprint rework \cite{inayat2015systematic}. To mitigate these issues without incurring heavy documentation overhead, we propose an Enriched User Story workflow \cite{acm2022proposal}. In this workflow, requirements analysts and UI/UX designers pair low-fidelity interactive wireframes directly with user stories during backlog grooming. 

By grounding textual acceptance criteria in concrete visual components, development teams can systematically detect and eliminate the four classes of requirements ambiguity identified by Berry et al. \cite{berry2003contract}: (1) lexical ambiguities are clarified through standardized interface labels, (2) syntactic conditionals are verified via visible decision paths, (3) scope boundaries are constrained by explicit form fields, and (4) subjective vagueness is replaced by measurable visual inputs. For quality attributes that cannot be visualized directly, teams can augment wireframe walkthroughs with lightweight think-aloud prompts \cite{abad2018loud} to capture latency, security, and exception-handling expectations early in the sprint lifecycle.

\subsection{Preliminary pilot validation in a capstone project}
To evaluate the practical efficacy of this proposed workflow, we conducted a preliminary pilot experiment on ten real-world user stories from an undergraduate software engineering project developing a Self-Storage Facility Rental and Management System. Requirements ambiguity was evaluated across the four linguistic categories established by Berry et al. \cite{berry2003contract}. In the baseline textual user stories, fifteen distinct ambiguity defects were identified across all ten statements (100\% defect rate). 

Following the introduction of low-fidelity interactive wireframes and visual acceptance criteria, fourteen ambiguities were successfully eliminated, achieving an Ambiguity Resolution Rate of $ARR = 93.3\%$ (structural, scope, and anaphoric ambiguities were reduced by 100\%). A non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank test confirmed a statistically significant reduction in requirement defects ($Z = -2.81, p = 0.005 < 0.01$). Crucially, the single unresolved defect involved transaction latency, empirically corroborating the non-functional requirement deficit of static visual prototypes \cite{rueda2020requirements} and highlighting the necessity of combining wireframes with explicit quality attribute checklists.
